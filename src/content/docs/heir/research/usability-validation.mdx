<p class="page-deck"><em>Did the redesigned information architecture improve task completion, efficiency, and perceived ease?</em></p>

The redesigned prototype was compared with the old app using the same four
representative tasks. The old study included **50 participants** and the
redesigned study included **60 participants**.

The tasks exercised the parts of the redesign most relevant to the earlier
information-architecture work: goals, vibration controls, activity insights,
and notification controls.

The broad pattern is clear: the redesign produced **higher task completion,
higher perceived ease, fewer interactions, and a much higher System Usability
Scale score**. Median average task time improved modestly overall because one
task moved in the opposite direction.

## 1. Did users complete more tasks?

<StatisticalPlot src={chart0} alt="Task success increased across all four tasks" />

The redesign raised average participant task success from **70.0% to 90.4%**
(**p &lt; 0.001**). The clearest task-level gains were **Adjust vibration
intensity** (74% → 95%; adjusted p = 0.005) and **Turn off vibration alerts**
(60% → 95%; adjusted p &lt; 0.001).

**Check activity insights** also improved from 66% to 83%, but that difference
did not remain statistically clear after correcting for the four task
comparisons. **Set step goals** rose from 80% to 88% without a clear statistical
difference.

## 2. Did the redesign reduce navigation effort?

<StatisticalPlot src={chart1} alt="Most redesigned tasks required less effort" />

The redesigned structure substantially reduced interaction effort for the three
tasks most directly tied to navigation:

- **Vibration intensity:** median clicks fell from 20 to 7.
- **Activity insights:** median clicks fell from 31 to 11, while median time
  fell from 56.7 to 25.9 seconds.
- **Turning off alerts:** median clicks fell from 28 to 4, while median time
  fell from 23.2 to 9.2 seconds.

**Set step goals was the exception.** Its median completion time increased from
13.7 to 40.8 seconds, even though success increased slightly and median clicks
fell from 16 to 13. The redesigned goal-setting flow therefore remained the
clearest efficiency problem.

## 3. Did tasks feel as easy as participants expected?

Before each task, participants rated how easy they expected it to be. After the
task, they rated experienced ease using the 1–7 Single Ease Question (SEQ).

<StatisticalPlot src={chart2} alt="The redesign largely closed the gap between expected and experienced ease" />

In the old app, participants expected all four tasks to be relatively easy, but
the experience fell well short—especially for vibration intensity, insights,
and stopping alerts. In the redesign, those three gaps fell close to zero.

**Set step goals again stands out:** the expectation gap improved only modestly,
which is consistent with its longer completion time.

## 4. Did the app feel more usable overall?

<StatisticalPlot src={chart3} alt="Overall perceived usability rose sharply" />

Mean SUS increased from **47.5 to 75.8** (**p &lt; 0.001**). This is consistent
with the task-level pattern: the redesign was not merely easier to complete;
participants also experienced the system as substantially more usable overall.

## 5. What does this validate from the IA redesign?

The usability results provide the strongest follow-through on the earlier card
sort and tree test around **Settings**.

The two tasks most directly related to the earlier Device Settings–Customize
ambiguity improved substantially after those controls were consolidated into
the redesigned Settings structure. **Adjust vibration intensity reached 95%
success, with median clicks falling from 20 to 7 and SEQ increasing from 3.3 to
5.9. Turning off vibration alerts also reached 95% success, with median clicks
falling from 28 to 4 and SEQ increasing from 2.3 to 5.6.**

The redesigned **Insights** destination also performed considerably better:
success increased from 66% to 83%, median completion time was cut by more than
half, and SEQ increased from 2.7 to 4.6.

The redesign therefore appears to have resolved much of the navigation friction
identified in the earlier IA research. **Goal setting remained the clearest
exception**: users generally completed it successfully, but the redesigned flow
was slower and did not feel meaningfully easier.

### Method note

The old and redesigned studies are treated as **independent samples**. Task
success is based on PASS/SKIP status. Time and click comparisons use medians in
the report because the task-level distributions are skewed. SEQ and pre-task
ease use 1–7 scales; their difference is shown descriptively as an expectation
gap.

Task-success significance checks use Fisher's exact tests with
Benjamini–Hochberg correction across the four tasks. SUS is compared with a
Welch two-sample t-test.

Three redesigned participants are missing the click count for the
"Turn off vibration alerts" task; those values remain missing rather than being
treated as zero.

Exploratory novice-versus-experienced wearable-user comparisons did not show a
clear redesign-by-experience interaction across the five overall usability
measures, so that segmentation is not emphasized in the main report.
