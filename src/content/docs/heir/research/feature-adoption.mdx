This report summarizes **1,800 users** and
**140,043 feature records** through
**September 26, 2026**.

## 1. Which features caught on?

<StatisticalPlot src={chart0} alt="App notifications show the strongest adoption" />

App notifications lead at every reference point. Silent reminders have the
clearest adoption gap. First-week alarm activation is
**25.6 percentage points** higher than reminder
activation (**McNemar p &lt;0.001**).

## 2. How broadly are notifications configured?

<StatisticalPlot src={chart1} alt="Most users keep notifications to a relatively small set of apps" />

<StatisticalPlot src={chart2} alt="The most commonly enabled apps still reach a minority of all users" />

The notification experience is broad in reach but selective in configuration:
most users do not enable alerts from a large number of apps.

## 3. What happens after a feature is activated?

<StatisticalPlot src={chart3} alt="Once activated, all three features are usually still enabled at day 45" />

The contrast is clearest for reminders: relatively few users activate them, but
those who do generally keep them configured.

**App notifications** combine broad adoption with strong persistence.
**Silent alarms** show moderate adoption and similarly strong persistence after
activation. **Silent reminders** have much lower initial uptake, but their high
post-activation persistence suggests that the main gap is getting users to adopt
the feature in the first place.

### Method note

Configuration is reconstructed from successful setting changes and snapshots.
For app notifications, an app counts as enabled only while both its app toggle
and the notification master switch are on. Seven-day activation uses equal
follow-up windows; day-30/day-45 retention uses the same 45-day-mature activators.
